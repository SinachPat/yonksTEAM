## 1. Executive Summary

### 1.1 The Core Positioning

**YonksTEAM is the brand of Jason and Tyler Younker, a married duo who help successful but burned-out CPAs, financial advisors, and high-performing professionals get their lives back from the careers that own them.**

This is not a real estate company. It is not a crypto venture. It is not an AI tool. The past ventures — REtoken, BMT Guide, the investment clubs, even Retken — were chapters in Jason and Tyler's own journey of building freedom. The current website tells the *story of their journey*. The new website must tell the story of **what that journey means for the person visiting the site** — the burned-out advisor who needs to hear that there's a way out.

### 1.2 The Controlling Idea (Single Sentence)

> Jason and Tyler Younker are a married duo who help successful but burned-out CPAs and financial advisors get their lives back from the practices that own them.

### 1.3 The Transformation Arc

| From | To |
|------|----|
| The perfect life on paper, and done inside | A life that feels as good as it looks |
| An advisor with everything and nothing left inside | An advisor who wants the day ahead again |
| Stuck between hating the work and having no good way to leave it | The advisor who got their life back and left the practice in the hands of the people who love it |

### 1.4 Key Objectives

| Objective | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Credibility** | Establish Jason & Tyler as the definitive guides for burned-out advisors — lived experience, professional credentials, proven model |
| **Empathy First** | Make the visitor feel seen and understood before any pitch. They've been quiet too long. Someone finally gets it. |
| **Clear Path Forward** | The 3-step plan (Say it / Picture it / Wake up wanting the day) must be unmistakable |
| **Conversion** | Drive two CTAs: "Start the conversation" (direct) and "Join the newsletter" (transitional) |
| **Product Awareness** | Introduce Exit to Client as the flagship offering for advisors who want to leave the practice in client hands |

### 1.5 Success Metrics

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| Page Load Time | < 2s | Professional audience expects speed |
| Bounce Rate | < 35% | Content must resonate immediately with burned-out advisors |
| "Start the Conversation" Clicks | Measurable | Primary conversion event |
| Newsletter Sign-ups | Measurable | Transitional conversion — builds the list |
| Time on Page | > 3 min | The story needs time to land |
| Scroll Depth | > 70% | The full arc must be consumed |

## 3. Target Audiences

### 3.1 Primary Audience: The Burned-Out Advisor

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Role | CPA or financial advisor, 25–30 years into a successful practice |
| External Reality | The practice cannot run a week without them. Slowing down means income slows down. |
| Internal Reality | They hate work they used to love. They can't say that out loud. Complaining about a successful practice sounds ungrateful. |
| The Fear | AI headlines. Practice value dropping. The only exit on offer is selling to a stranger who'll scatter their clients. |
| The Quiet Hope | That someone will tell them it's okay to want out — and show them how. |

### 3.2 Secondary Audience: The Burned-Out Professional

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Role | High performer, 20–30 years into a successful career (corporate seat, partnership, professional practice) |
| External Reality | Everything runs through them at full throttle |
| Internal Reality | Same as the advisor — but the guilt is even more abstract. "I have a great career. Why am I miserable?" |
| Key Difference | May not have a "practice to sell" — but has a life to reclaim |

### 3.3 Audience Hierarchy on the Site

The site should speak primarily to the **Burned-Out Advisor** (the most specific, highest-value target) and allow the **Burned-Out Professional** to see themselves in the story. The brand strategy doc gives us three variants of the same core message:

| Variant | Controlling Idea | Best Used For |
|---------|-----------------|---------------|
| Burned-Out Advisor | "Get your life back from the practice that owns you" | Homepage hero, primary messaging |
| Advisor + Exit Path | "Rebuild or sell to clients — either way, you get your life back" | Exit to Client page, practice-specific pages |
| Burned-Out Professional | "Get your life back from the career that owns you" | Secondary messaging, social, blog, newsletter |

The old site was a single page about *them*. The new site needs to be about *the visitor* — and then about how Jason & Tyler are the answer.

| About | Jason & Tyler's story — but framed as *the story that qualifies them to guide you* |
| Newsletter | "The Next Season" — one list, from the two of them, about the next season |
| Blog | Building credibility, showing the thinking, case studies |
| Contact | "Start the conversation" — low-friction, high-trust |

## 5. Page-by-Page Content Blueprint

### 5.1 Homepage (`/`)

**Purpose:** The visitor lands and immediately thinks "That's me. Someone finally gets it."

#### 5.1.1 Hero Section

| Element | Content | Rationale |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Headline | **You hate work you used to love.** | The most honest thing they've never said out loud. Immediate recognition. |
| Subheadline | You can say that here. | Permission. The second sentence that makes them breathe. |
| Supporting Text | You built a successful practice. It was supposed to fund your life — not be the thing that keeps you from living it. You're not ungrateful. You're trapped. And we know exactly how to get you out. | Naming the internal conflict, the philosophical problem, and the promise of a solution — all in three sentences. |
| Primary CTA | "Start the Conversation →" | To `/contact` |
| Secondary CTA | "Read Our Story →" | To `/about` — for visitors who need to vet who these people are |

**Visual:** A warm, human photo of Jason & Tyler together. Not corporate headshots. Candid, genuine, the kind of people you'd trust with a hard conversation.

#### 5.1.2 The Recognition Section (The Problem)

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Headline | **A practice you built to fund your life shouldn't be the thing keeping you from living it.** |
| Body | If you're a CPA or financial advisor 25 years into a successful practice, there's a good chance this sounds familiar: The phone rings all day. The practice can't run a week without you. You can't slow down without the income slowing too. And the work? You used to love it. Now you're not sure you even like it. But complaining about a successful practice sounds ungrateful, so you keep quiet and keep going. |

**Design note:** This section should feel spacious. No competing visuals. Just the visitor and the truth they've been avoiding.

#### 5.1.3 The Empathy Section (We've Been There)

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Headline | **We know exactly what that feels like.** |
| Tyler's Story | Tyler spent eight years in sales chasing a number nobody in company history had ever hit. The year she finally hit it, she wanted a marching band down the hall, cheerleaders, glitter falling from the sky. She got a congratulations email and a nice lunch. And in January, her ticker flipped back to zero. |
| Jason's Story | Jason burned out on his IT career in 2017. He started driving Uber just to buy back a little freedom. |
| Connecting Line | We know what it's like to have everything and hate your life anyway. We also know what it's like to get out. |

**Visual:** Split photo or side-by-side candid shots of Jason and Tyler — authentic, not staged.

#### 5.1.4 The Authority Section (Who We Are)

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Headline | **The way out we teach is the way we took.** |
| Credential Block 1 | Eight years ago, we traded careers for income-producing assets. We've been full-time travelers ever since, raising a five-year-old who thinks it's normal to ask which city she'll live in tomorrow. |
| Credential Block 2 | Before that season, Tyler raised $3 billion institutionally — her clients were financial advisors and stockbrokers, never retail investors. Today she serves as a FINRA arbitrator. |
| Credential Block 3 | Jason is training as a Certified Exit Planning Advisor (CEPA). We've spent the years since our own escape building ownership models designed to buy other people the same freedom. |

**Design note:** This section should feel like a conversation, not a résumé. Each credential is delivered in context, not in a bullet list.

#### 5.1.5 The Plan Section (The Way Out)

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Headline | **Here's how it works.** |
| Subheadline | Three steps. Not a program. A path. |

**Step 1: Say it out loud.**
Admit how you really feel about the thing you built — to people who have felt it too. This is the hardest step, and the one you can't do alone.

**Step 2: Picture what replaces it.**
Get concrete about the life you want next. What does it need to earn? Do you want the practice rebuilt around that life — or do you want to walk away? If you want to walk away, Exit to Client lets you sell to the people who already know exactly what it's worth: your clients.

**Step 3: Wake up wanting the day.**
Every change we help you make points at that one outcome. Not a retirement number. Not a valuation multiple. A day you actually want to show up for.

#### 5.1.6 The Success/Failure Section

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Headline | **What winning looks like. What losing looks like.** |
| Success | You stop dreading the phone. Your week fits around your life instead of the other way around. Your income stops depending on your calendar. You can answer "what's next" with something you can't wait to start. When you do hand the practice off, it happens on your terms and your timeline. |
| Failure | Keep white-knuckling toward a retirement date that keeps moving. Let the practice keep eating evenings and weekends until health or family makes the decision for you. Sell in a hurry to whoever shows up with a check, and watch thirty years of client relationships get scattered. Keep hating it quietly while everyone keeps congratulating you. |

**Design note:** This should be presented as two columns or a visual split. Success on one side, failure on the other. Not scare tactics — but honest clarity.

#### 5.1.7 The Transformation Section

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Headline | **From: The perfect life on paper, and done inside.** |
| To: | **A life that feels as good as it looks.** |
| Body | This is what we do. We help you cross that gap. Not with a framework. Not with a workbook. With a conversation, a plan, and a partner who's already been where you are. |

#### 5.1.8 Final CTA Section

| Element | Content |
|---------|---------|
| Headline | **Ready to want the day again?** |
| CTA 1 | "Start the Conversation →" |
| CTA 2 | "Join the Newsletter →" |
| Tagline | *One list. From the two of us. About the next season.* |

## 6. Voice & Tone Guidelines

### 6.1 Brand Voice

| Attribute | Description | Do This | Avoid This |
|-----------|-------------|---------|------------|
| **Honest** | Says the thing nobody says | "You hate work you used to love." | "Optimize your professional fulfillment." |
| **Warm** | Human, inviting, not clinical | "We know what that feels like." | "Our proprietary methodology addresses..." |
| **Authoritative** | Earned credentials, not bragging | Tyler raised $3B institutionally. | "We're industry-leading experts." |
| **Direct** | No fluff, no filler | "Here's how it works. Three steps." | "Let's explore the myriad pathways available..." |
| **Personal** | First-person, real stories | "Jason burned out on his IT career..." | "The founding team experienced career fatigue..." |

### 6.2 Tone Spectrum

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Hero / Headlines | Unflinching, provocative | "You hate work you used to love." |
| The Problem | Honest, validating | "You can't say that out loud. Complaining sounds ungrateful." |
| The Empathy | Warm, personal, story-driven | "Tyler spent eight years chasing a number nobody had ever hit." |
| The Authority | Confident, earned | "She raised $3 billion institutionally. Today she's a FINRA arbitrator." |
| The Plan | Clear, simple, direct | "Step 1: Say it out loud." |
| The CTA | Inviting, low-pressure | "Start the conversation. We'll respond with honesty." |

### 6.3 Words to Use & Words to Avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| Burned out, trapped, stuck | Disengaged, underwhelmed, suboptimal |
| Hate work you used to love | Career dissatisfaction, professional fatigue |
| Get your life back | Achieve work-life balance, reclaim agency |
| Say it out loud | Open up, be vulnerable |
| The way out | The solution, the framework |
| Practice, career | Business, enterprise |
| Clients, people | Customers, stakeholders |
| The next season | The next chapter, phase two |

### 6.4 The "YonksTEAM Voice" Checklist

Before publishing any content, ask:
- [ ] Would a burned-out advisor read this and feel *seen*?
- [ ] Does it sound like a real person wrote it? (Read it aloud.)
- [ ] Is every credential earned in context, not dropped in a list?
- [ ] Is the jargon gone? (Would they need to Google anything?)
- [ ] Does it give permission, not instruction?
- [ ] Is the CTA the natural next step, not a hard sell?

## 7. Content Patterns & Components

### 7.1 Reusable Content Components

| Component | Description | Used On |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Hero Block** | Provocative headline + validating subheadline + 1–2 CTAs | All pages |
| **Recognition Block** | Naming the problem the visitor feels but hasn't said | Homepage, For Advisors |
| **Empathy Story** | Personal story from Jason or Tyler that mirrors the visitor's experience | Homepage, About |
| **Authority Block** | Credential delivered in context, not as a bullet list | Homepage, About |
| **Plan Step** | Numbered step with clear title and 1–2 sentence explanation | Homepage, For Advisors |
| **Success/Failure Split** | Two-column visual of what winning and losing look like | Homepage |
| **Transformation Statement** | From/To — the core brand promise | Homepage, For Advisors |
| **CTA Section** | Headline + 1–2 buttons | Bottom of most pages |
| **Two Paths** | Visual comparison of two options (rebuild vs. exit) | For Advisors, Exit to Client |
| **Comparison Table** | Traditional exit vs. Exit to Client | Exit to Client |

### 7.2 Content Hierarchy Rules

Every page should follow this structure:

1. **Hook** (Hero) — The unflinching truth they haven't heard anywhere else
2. **Recognition** — "That's me." The problem named.
3. **Empathy** — "We've been there." Stories that prove it.
4. **Authority** — "We know what we're talking about." Credentials earned in context.
5. **The Way Out** — The plan. Concrete, clear, 3 steps.
6. **Action** — "Start the conversation."

### 7.3 Microcopy Guidelines

| Element | Guideline | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Buttons | Start with a verb, be direct | "Start the Conversation" not "Learn More" |
| Form labels | Conversational, direct | "What's on your mind?" not "Message:" |
| Error messages | Warm, human | "That email didn't work. Try again?" |
| Empty states | N/A (this isn't an app) | — |
| Loading states | Honest, simple | "Sending..." |

## 9. Visual + Content Integration Notes

### 9.1 Design Principles for Content

| Principle | Content Implication |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Permission** | The design should feel like a relief — spacious, quiet, warm. Not loud, flashy, or salesy. |
| **Trust** | Photos of real people. No stock photography. Every visual should feel like a conversation. |
| **Clarity** | One idea per section. No competing visuals. The words are the most important thing. |
| **Pace** | Short paragraphs. Lots of whitespace. The visitor should feel like they can breathe. |
| **Mobile-first** | Every content block must work on a phone. The story must survive a small screen. |

### 9.2 Visual Content Inventory

| Asset Needed | Type | Priority | Notes |
|-------------|------|----------|-------|
| Jason & Tyler — candid, warm | Photo | High | For the hero. Should feel like a conversation, not a headshot. |
| Jason & Tyler — on the road (travel life) | Photo | High | For the about page — shows the reality of the freedom they built. |
| Jason & Tyler — event speaking | Photo | Medium | Credibility in context. |
| Brand mark / logo | Graphic | High | Simple, clean, professional. |
| Before/After visual (From/To) | Illustration | Medium | Optional — the transformation statement may work better as text. |
| Family / travel lifestyle photos | Photo | Low | For the about page, blog, newsletter. |

### 9.3 Color Palette Direction

| Color | Usage | Feeling |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Deep navy or charcoal | Headlines, primary text | Serious, professional, trustworthy |
| Warm accent (amber or terracotta) | CTAs, highlights, emphasis | Warmth, humanity, approachability |
| Off-white / cream | Background | Warm, inviting — not sterile white |
| Light gray | Card backgrounds, subtle sections | Depth without distraction |

### 9.4 Typography

| Usage | Recommendation | Rationale |
|-------|----------------|-----------|
| Headlines | Serif or semi-serif (e.g., Georgia, Charter, Tiempos) | Warmth, authority, readability |
| Body | Sans-serif (e.g., Inter, Source Sans) | Clean, modern, highly readable on screen |
| Tone | Generous leading (1.6–1.8 line height) | The content needs room to breathe |
